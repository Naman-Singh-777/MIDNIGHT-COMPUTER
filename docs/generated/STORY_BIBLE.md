# Story bible

Setting: Vesper Hollow Sanatorium, October 1963, night intake. Rain, one lamp, a glass window onto an unheated hall, a ledger terminal that predates the building's wiring.

## Why you are here

Your mother, Ada Wren, has been on Ward B since April. She is in bed 9. Night staff may sit with family at six, so you took the night intake window to be near her. Everyone you admit sleeps on her ward. The goal for the night is plain: keep the Understudy off Ward B until six, then go and see her. The ledger has her as confined to the ward, so when she turns up at the glass at 01:52, the paperwork is the only thing telling you it is not her.

What the game is about: the thing that replaces people only needs you to stop checking, and the person you most want to see is the one you will check least.

## Pell's list

Like the to-do list in Shift at Midnight, the night has jobs away from the glass. Patients keep arriving while you do them, so every job is a choice about leaving the window.

| Job | Where | Window | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mop the corridor | Stain by the bucket, east corridor | 22:00 to 22:45 | Hold E and scrub with the mouse. The stain drags toward the Ward B door and has a handprint in it. Halfway through, something tries the Ward B handle behind you |
| File last week's slips | Filing cabinet in the booth | 22:00 to 22:45 | Sort six index cards into three drawers. The last card is yours, admitted tonight at 22:00, Ward B bed 10, pencilled "not yet" |
| 00:30 head count | Slot in the Ward B door | 00:30 to 01:10 | Torch through a letterbox, click each person once. Every Understudy you admitted is standing in the aisle. Look at one too long and it is at the slot |
| Dawn | Same slot | 05:46 to 06:00 | The ending, seen through the slot |

Missed jobs cost nerves and get a call from the Matron's office or Pell.

## Three layers

**Surface.** You are the night intake officer. People come to the glass with a slip. You check the slip against the ledger, ask five questions, and decide. Some of them are not people.

**Middle.** Something called the Understudy has been learning the building. It does not attack. It applies. It copies a person who was already admitted, arrives with their paperwork slightly wrong, and gets better every time you let it through or turn it away. Ward B keeps counting one extra patient. The blankets in the ward are folded. Nobody on the ward folds blankets.

**Deep.** The Understudy learned to talk from you. It repeats your words because you are the only one who has spoken to it all night. Entry 000 in the ledger is in your handwriting, dated tonight at 22:00. The officer was admitted before the shift began. Sister Imogen's voice on the phone is what has kept the officer calm enough to work. When the voice at the gate says her word, the question is whether the person who has been talking to you all night was real.

## Safe place, subverted

The booth is warm and the lamp is honest for the first half of the night. After minute 135 the lamp can flicker and the room can go quiet around a stare. The booth never becomes a monster, it just stops being a place where nothing happens.

## Consequences, so choices have weight

Admit a mimic: the ward calls you later and a stage goes up. Hold a mimic: the observation room is found open, door unforced. Refuse a mimic: it returns at the window knowing what you asked. Refuse a human: they come back soaked and quiet. Contain a human: guilt, and a whisper you did not ask for. Contain a mimic: a clean catch and a little of your nerve back.

## Endings (built, shown through the Ward B slot at dawn)

1. Clean: nothing got through. She turns her head and says "There you are."
2. Crowded: Understudies got through. She is asleep, and the extra ones sit up around her bed, breathing when she breathes.
3. Taken: you let her copy in at 01:52. Bed 9 is made, the blanket folded. Someone says your name in her voice behind you.
4. Absent: you never went to the slot. Bed 9 is stripped by morning.

All four close on Entry 000 in your handwriting.

## Voice guide

Plain sentences. Staff speak like tired professionals, not villains. Nobody explains the Understudy. The horror is in the paperwork and in the way a person answers a question one beat too early.
